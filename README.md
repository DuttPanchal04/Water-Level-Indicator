# 💧 Water Level Indicator

A simple yet effective water level monitoring system using **BC547 transistors**, LEDs, and a buzzer to indicate different water levels in a container. This project helps detect low, medium, and full levels with visual and audio alerts.

---

## 🌟 Overview

This project demonstrates a water level indicator circuit using three BC547 transistors and three LEDs to indicate low, half, and full levels of water in a container. A buzzer sounds an alert when the water reaches the top level, helping prevent overflow or wastage.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

- Understand how to detect water levels using basic electronic components.
- Design a low-cost water level alert system.
- Learn the switching behavior of transistors when used in sensing circuits.

## 📐 Circuit Description

- Transistors act as electronic switches that are turned ON by current flowing through water.
- Each transistor's **base** is dipped into the container at different levels.
- As the water level rises, current flows through water into each transistor's base, turning it ON and lighting the corresponding LED.
- The final transistor activates a **buzzer** when the water reaches the full level.

---

## 🛠️ How It Works

1. Initially, all transistors are OFF — no LEDs or buzzer are active.
2. As water touches the first base sensor, the first transistor activates and lights up LED 1.
3. As the water rises:
   - LED 2 glows at half level.
   - At full level, transistor Q3 switches the **buzzer** ON and provides an audio alert.

This mechanism simulates how water can be used as a conductive path to activate transistor-based logic.

## ✅ Advantages

- Simple, low-cost design
- Easy to install and use
- Visual and audio alert system
- No programming required
- Compact and low maintenance

## ⚠️ Disadvantages

- No long-term warranty
- Sensors may degrade over time (approx. every 3 years)

## 🏭 Applications

- Household water tanks
- Industrial liquid level monitoring
- Sea-level or tsunami warning systems
- Sump pit or rainwater monitoring
